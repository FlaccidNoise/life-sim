# Tidepool M4 — Teeth

Status: **Draft. It waits for the owner. Build nothing from it.**

**Goal:** Some bodies carry a Jaw and live in part on other bodies. Prey carry spikes, shells, and
toxin, and escape with a dash. Predators and prey coexist at tick 12,000 on 3 seeds, and the long
gate reads the same at tick 144,000. A body that dies of a bite stops the world on its killer, and the
death card shows the killer's brain at the kill.

**Architecture:** Four new part kinds: Jaw, Spike, Shell plate, Toxin gland. A Jaw bites a body that
it touches and faces. A Spike and a Shell plate guard the arc of the outline where they sit. Three
abilities come from parts: dash (Flagellum), lunge (Jaw), toxin cloud (Toxin gland). Each is one
brain output that the part declares. A bite moves energy from the bitten body to the biter and makes
none. Every death leaves a piece of debris, and a Filter eats debris as it eats plants. The page draws
the new parts, debris, and clouds, gives the player ability buttons, and turns the death screen into a
card for the killer. Full detail: `../architecture.md`.

**Tech stack:** unchanged from M3. Rust 1.99.0, TypeScript 6, Vite 8, Vitest 4, ESLint 10, pnpm 11,
Preact 11.

**Read first:** `../../AGENTS.md`, `../findings/m3-tuning.md`, `../findings/m3-brains.md`,
`../findings/m3-inputs.md`, `../findings/m1-density.md`, `../decisions/INDEX.md`, `../design.md` §5,
§6, and §13, `../research/draft-inventory.md` section 4 C (C9 to C16) and C17, `../gates.md`.

**Blocked by:** the M3 exit (Task 15 of `2026-10-13-m3-minds.md`) and the owner's approval of this
plan. At the head of this draft (`f0f2066`) `mix_rate` is still 0, so stage 2 of M3 is open. Every row
that this plan calls "the M3 rows" is the final proof of the M3 exit, and it is not known yet.

## How predation gets past the draft's encounter failure

The draft: at 8192 square with 600 to 850 creatures, the nearest neighbour of a prey was 73 to 86 units
away against a bite range of 16. No bite landed on any seed (`research/draft-inventory.md:411-415`,
C1). Raising the reach overshot: 12 killed predators, 30 filled the cap, 50 made an all-predator world
(`:427-429`, C4).

What Tidepool has instead, from its own readings:

- **The world is about 3.5 times closer, in units of reach.** On the M1 world at tick 12,000 the median
  distance from a creature to its nearest creature was 9.7 units (`m1-density.md`, seed 1, 1,477
  creatures). The reach of a contact is the sum of two radii, and a radius is `(length + width) / 4`
  (`crates/sim/src/creature.rs:152`). At tick 144,000 of M3, `len` reads 9.7 to 11.4 and `area` 38 to
  46 (`m3-tuning.md`, the close of stage 1), so a width of about 4 and a contact reach of about 7. That
  is 9.7 against 7: a ratio of about 1.4. The draft's ratio was 73 to 86 against 16: about 5.
- **Bodies clump on 14 fertile patches,** and a child is born touching its parent
  (`crates/sim/src/repro.rs:100-103`).
- **The bite is a contact.** A Jaw bites a body whose circle overlaps its own and that sits inside its
  cone (rule J1). The reach comes from the body, as C4 asks, and is not a free knob. A body with more
  reach is a larger body, which pays for its size.
- **The motor already finds every overlap** (`crates/sim/src/motor.rs:24-27`, `touch`). The bite
  target is found in the same loop.

What is not measured: the 9.7 is one seed of the M1 world, where bodies were 6.6 long. Nobody has
measured how often a body of the M3 world touches another, how long a contact lasts, or how often the
other body sits in front of it. `design.md` §7.3 says a headless probe checks encounter rates before
any ecology tuning starts. **Task 1 is that probe, on the head of M3, before any rule is built.** Its
rows go to the owner with this plan's first build report. If they show that a frontal contact is rare,
density is the first suspect, and density is set by the food. The draft found that more food killed
predators (C8), so the fix is not more food. It is a finding for the owner.

## How the gate reads "predators and prey coexist"

A **hunter** is a body with a Jaw. **Prey** is a body with none. Flesh is energy that a bite moved.

`gate-health` prints five more measures on every row. Each is read every 500 ticks over the second
half of the run (choice C6).

| Measure | Meaning | What it rejects |
|---|---|---|
| `hunt_lo` | Least share of the living that are hunters | Hunters die out |
| `hunt_hi` | Most share of the living that are hunters | Hunters take over, also in a boom that later busts |
| `prey_lo` | Least count of living prey | Prey eaten out |
| `kills_late` | Deaths by a bite in the last 2,000 ticks | A world where jaws are carried and never kill |
| `flesh_late` | Flesh over all energy eaten (plants, debris, flesh) in the last 2,000 ticks | The same, by energy, and a world where kills happen but feed nothing |

Why five and not one: a Jaw body keeps its Filter (choice C3), so a world where bites do nothing can
still hold jaws. The share of hunters alone would pass it. `kills_late` and `flesh_late` read that the
jaws are used.

**The bad inputs that prove the bands** (Task 11, run before the good world is looked at):

| Band | Bad world | Why it is bad |
|---|---|---|
| `kills_late`, `flesh_late` | `--param bite_rate=0` with every other Start value | No predation. Jaws are dead weight on an omnivore |
| `prey_lo`, `hunt_hi` | `--param founder_jaw=1 --param bite_mass_ratio=0` | Every founder hunts and any body can be bitten: predators eat everything they can reach |
| `hunt_lo` | `--param founder_jaw=0` | Jaws come only from the add-part mutation. The draft: a guild absent at founding does not appear (C10) |

Each band is set between the bad rows and the good rows. If no value separates them, the row says
"not a gate" and goes to the owner. The bands come to the owner as provisional, as in M3.

**Why 12,000 ticks is not enough alone:** see choice C6. The 12,000 reading is the exit's words and
runs in CI. The 144,000 reading on seeds 1, 4, 5, 6, 8 is where Tidepool has lost what it had, twice.
The plan claims coexistence only where both pass.

## The throughput budget

The floor is 2,400 ticks per second: 417 microseconds per tick. The head of M3 reads 2,702 to 2,835 in
undisturbed rounds: 353 to 370 microseconds, at 1,357 creatures. The room to the floor is 47 to 64
microseconds. The room to the M3 target of 2,700 is 0 to 17.

What M4 adds to a tick, and what is known of its cost:

| Added work | Size | Cost known? |
|---|---|---|
| The jaw cone test in the motor's overlap loop | Only for a body with a jaw, only for bodies it overlaps | No |
| `bite::step` | One pass over the slots. Work only for a body with a target | No |
| A Filter scans the debris grid | One more grid query per creature per tick, beside the plant query of `energy::contact` | No. Likely the largest |
| Debris and cloud decay | One pass over each pool | No |
| `ability::step` | One pass over the slots | No |
| Toxin clouds | One grid query per live cloud per tick, at most 256 clouds | No |
| More brain neurons | A founder goes from 13 inputs and 3 outputs to about 18 and 4, plus the hand-wired connections of rule P2. About 1.4 ns per connection per tick (`research/kernel-bench.md`) | Arithmetic only: 10 more connections per brain is about 9 microseconds per tick at 660 brain steps |
| A population change | Predation lowers it, debris raises it | No. This can move the bench either way |

So:

- Every rule task reads `node scripts/bench-wasm.mjs` with its rule at Start, against the reading of
  Task 8, at the same load. A task that costs more than 5% stops and reports.
- The bench prints one more value: nanoseconds per creature-tick, `1e9 / (tps * creatures)`. It lets
  a thinner world be compared with a fuller one (choice C9).
- Target after the retune: 2,700 or more in WASM at the reference world, or the owner's answer to C9.
- Levers, in order: a Filter scans the debris on think ticks only, the debris floor up, the debris pool
  down. `MAX_CONN_GENES` down to 192 is a budget and goes to the owner first, as in M3.
- If the reading is under 2,400, stop and report.

## How the build runs without the owner

Proposed, as M3's was decided on 2026-10-13. The plan stops for the owner at five points: the motor
stop of Task 10, the bands of Task 11, the end of stage 1 in Task 12, the end of stage 2, and the exit
checks of Task 15. The build does not wait at the first three.

- At each of those stops the orchestrator takes the recommended option, logs it in
  `../decisions/INDEX.md` as "provisional, owner to confirm", and goes on.
- **Hard stops:** a change to an existing band, a rule, the floor, or the reference population.
  Deleting a feature. A target that is not met after its trial cap. `kills_late` of 0 on any seed when
  stage 1 switches on: that is the encounter failure of the draft, and its fix is a change to the world.
  A bench reading that is void (choice C9). Any push.
- No feature is added beyond the roadmap. Within-life learning stays where the roadmap has it.

## Decisions this plan carries

All in `../decisions/INDEX.md`. Do not reopen one without new evidence.

1. Evolution only. Nothing in a brain changes within a life. "Learned" in the exit sentence means
   evolved.
2. A Filter eats on contact with no brain output.
3. A part has no upkeep of its own. It costs through its mass.
4. Brain upkeep: a fixed cost per body, plus a small cost per hidden neuron and per connection. An input
   and an output neuron cost nothing (M3 C1). So the new inputs and outputs of M4 cost nothing.
5. An Eye declares its inputs per gene (M3 C2).
6. The bands of a gate follow their proofs. A gate counts after it has rejected a bad input.
7. `make gates-long` runs at the exit, on seeds 1, 4, 5, 6, 8.
8. Parts ride in every frame record.
9. Every income stops at "full", and transfers conserve energy (`design.md` R5). Gain above 1 is
   ruled out by the draft (`research/draft-inventory.md:475-477`, C13).

## Differences from the roadmap and the design, to approve with this plan

1. **No `bite` output** (choice C1). A Jaw bites what it touches and faces. `lunge` is its trigger.
2. **An ability output is declared once per body, by kind,** not once per part gene. A body with any
   Flagellum declares `dash`, any Jaw `lunge`, any Toxin gland `toxin`. As the chemoreceptor's inputs
   are declared once (M3 C2).
3. **No taper gene** (choice C7). The outline follows the parts, as in look 4.
4. **The death card names the killer by its body, its hue, and its generation.** `design.md` §4 says
   "and its lineage". Lineage names are rivals, M7. The lineage tree is M5.
5. **Debris does not sink.** It lies where the body died. Sinking is M6.
6. **No audio.** The low cue of `design.md` §11 and of beat 11 is M8.
7. **A Spike and a Shell plate guard against bites only,** not against toxin.
8. **Abilities share one cooldown parameter,** `ability_cooldown`, with a cooldown per ability per body.

## The rules

One reference body: **the M3 founder with an eye.** Area 72 (`len` 12, `wid` 0.5), heft 1.065 with
its eye of 0.2, a mouth of `area / REF_WIDTH` = 12. One reference hunter: the same with a Jaw of 0.5
at the nose. Its part sum rises from 2.3 to 2.8, so its heft is `(1 + 2.8) / 3.1 = 1.226` and it is
1.15 times as heavy as the founder.

**K1. New kinds** (`genome.rs`).

```rust
pub const KIND_JAW: u8 = 7;
pub const KIND_SPIKE: u8 = 8;
pub const KIND_SHELL: u8 = 9;
pub const KIND_TOXIN: u8 = 10;
pub const KINDS: u8 = 10;
pub const SIZE_RANGE: [(f32, f32); 11] = [/* M3 rows */, (0.0, 1.0), (0.0, 1.0), (0.0, 1.0), (0.0, 1.0)];
/// The kinds that the add-part mutation draws from. 6 until Task 9, then KINDS.
pub const ADDABLE_KINDS: u8 = 6;
```

A part's direction is `anchor * PI` from the heading, as an eye's is. A midline part (anchor exactly
0 or 1) has one direction. Any other part is a pair at `+anchor * PI` and `-anchor * PI`.

`Creatures::express` makes, as it makes eye cones:

- **Jaw cones,** at most `MAX_JAWS` (4): direction cosine and sine, and size. `jaw_sum` is the sum of
  jaw sizes.
- **Guards,** at most `MAX_GUARDS` (8): direction cosine and sine, kind (Spike or Shell), and size.
  `shell_sum` is the sum of shell sizes.
- `gland_sum`, the sum of Toxin gland sizes.

A part past its cap still costs its mass.

**J1. The bite.** In `motor::step`, in the overlap loop of each body `s` with a jaw cone: an
overlapping body `j` is in cone `k` when `along > 0` and `along * along >= cos(jaw_arc)^2 * d2`, where
`along` is the offset to `j` along the cone's axis. No square root, as in `eye.rs`. The nearest such
`j` is kept in `Motor::bite_target[s]` (scratch, `NO_TOUCH` for none), and the sum of the sizes of the
cones that hold it in `Motor::bite_jaws[s]`.

`bite::step`, after `mate::step`. Each live slot `s` in ascending order, with a target `j`:

1. Skip if either body has energy 0 or less: it died earlier in this pass.
2. Skip unless `mass[s] >= bite_mass_ratio * mass[j]` (the motor's masses of this tick).
3. `room = energy_per_mass * area[s] - energy[s]`. Skip if `room <= 0`: a full body does not bite
   (`research/draft-inventory.md:470-473`, C12).
4. `bite = bite_rate * bite_jaws[s] * area[s] / REF_WIDTH`, times `lunge_bite` while `lunge_left[s]`
   is above 0.
5. The hit, in the frame of `j`: `h` is the offset from `j` to `s`, turned by minus the heading of `j`.
   `shell` and `spike` are the sums of the sizes of the guards of `j` of that kind whose arc holds `h`,
   by the same cone test with `shell_arc` and `spike_arc`.
6. `take = min(bite * (1 - min(1, shell_block * shell)), energy[j], room)`. `energy[j] -= take`,
   `energy[s] += take`. `stats.flesh += take`.
7. `hurt = spike_hurt * spike * bite`. `energy[s] -= hurt`. This energy is lost, as upkeep is.
8. `j` records the hit: `hurt[j] += take`, `hurt_side[j]` is the side of `h` (positive on the side of
   positive angles). `s` records `hurt` the same way, with the side of the spike.
9. A body at 0 or less is marked. Its killer is the other body.

After the loop, in slot order: `World::kill(slot, CAUSE_EATEN, killer)` for a bitten body,
`CAUSE_SPIKED` for a biter that a spike killed.

What moves is what one body loses and the other gains: rule J1 makes no energy. The two sinks are the
spike and the lost share of a corpse.

**D1. A Shell plate slows a body.** In `motor::step` the forward drag of a body is
`drag_fwd + shell_drag * shell_sum`, held under `SIDE_DRAG_MAX`.

**A1. Dash.** `ability::step` runs after `control::apply` and before `motor::step`. For each live slot
in ascending order, with `flag_push > 0`, `cmd_dash > 0`, and `cd_dash` at 0:
`cost = dash_cost * area / REF_WIDTH`. If `energy - cost > 0`: the energy is paid, the velocity gains
`dash_impulse * flag_push / heft` along the heading, `omega` gains
`dash_spin * cmd_turn * flag_push / heft`, and `cd_dash = ability_cooldown`. The motor's speed limit
still holds. The burst turn answers `design.md` R4 and the draft's C17
(`research/draft-inventory.md:493-498`): physical turning took prey evasion away there.

**A2. Lunge.** The same, with `jaw_sum > 0`, `cmd_lunge`, `cd_lunge`, `lunge_cost`, and a velocity gain
of `lunge_impulse * jaw_sum / heft`. It also sets `lunge_left = lunge_ticks`, which falls by 1 each
tick.

**A3. Toxin cloud.** The same, with `gland_sum > 0`, `cmd_toxin`, `cd_toxin`, `toxin_cost`, and a free
place in the cloud pool. A full pool fires nothing and charges nothing. The cloud: the body's centre,
radius `toxin_radius * length`, `toxin_ticks` to live, a drain of `toxin_drain * gland_sum`, and the
uid of its maker. `ability::clouds` runs after `bite::step`: each live cloud in pool order drains
`min(drain, energy)` from each body inside it but its maker, in grid order, and the energy is lost. A
body at 0 dies with `CAUSE_POISONED`, killed by the maker. Each cloud loses a tick and is freed at 0.

All three use the creature grid of the start of the tick, as `mate::step` and the eyes do.

**P1. Ports.** `brain::ports` is still the one place that knows.

```rust

pub const IN_HURT: u8 = 6;        // energy lost to bites, spikes, and toxin since the last think, times hurt_gain, held in 0 to 1
pub const IN_HURT_SIDE: u8 = 7;   // the side of the last hit: -1 to 1, 0 with no hit since the last think
pub const N_BODY_IN: usize = 8;
// One eye gene. A third input per cone.
pub const EYE_JAW_A: u8 = 4;      // the jaw size of the nearest body in cone A, 0 with none
pub const EYE_JAW_B: u8 = 5;
// Declared once by kind.
pub const OUT_DASH: u8 = 131;     // a body whose flagella sum to more than 0
pub const OUT_LUNGE: u8 = 132;    // a body with jaw_sum above 0
pub const OUT_TOXIN: u8 = 133;    // a body with gland_sum above 0
```

`OUT_DASH` is declared by a body whose flagella sum to more than 0, as the scent inputs are declared
by a chemoreceptor sum above 0. Input order: the 8 of the body, the scent, then the eye genes with
`FOOD, BODY, JAW` for cone A and then for cone B. Output order: thrust, turn, accept, then dash, lunge,
toxin for those declared. At most 6, under `N_OUT_MAX` (8).

`Brains::output` reads an output by port through an index per slot, since a part's output may be
absent. `brain::step` writes `cmd_dash`, `cmd_lunge`, `cmd_toxin` from the outputs, and -1 for one that
the body does not declare. `sense::step` writes the two hurt inputs and zeroes `hurt`. `eye::see`
writes three inputs per cone.

**P2. The starter brain.** Fixed weights, no new draw. The history ids follow those of M3.

- Every founder: each declared trigger output has bias `starter_trigger_bias`, so it rests closed.
  `IN_HURT` to `dash` at `starter_flee`. With an eye: `EYE_JAW_A` to `turn` at minus `starter_flee`
  and `EYE_JAW_B` at plus `starter_flee` (away from teeth), and both to `dash` at `starter_flee`.
- A hunter founder (rule P3) also: `EYE_BODY_A` to `turn` at `starter_chase` and `EYE_BODY_B` at minus
  `starter_chase` (toward a body, as `starter_look` turns toward a plant), and both to `lunge` at
  `starter_lunge`.

With every new starter weight at 0 the founder brain holds the M3 connections and the new ones at 0.
The walkthrough says that the founders' hunting and fleeing were wired by hand, as it says of their
foraging.

**P3. Hunter founders.** After each founder's genome and before its place, if `founder_jaw > 0`: one
draw from `rng_spawn`. Below `founder_jaw`, the founder gains a Jaw at anchor 0, size
`founder_jaw_size`, history `FOUNDER_JAW_HISTORY`, and keeps its Filter. Its place is drawn as every
founder's is, on fertile ground. With `founder_jaw` at 0 no draw is made.

**P4. A part that declares an output.** Group 5 of the mutation (add a part) draws its kind from
`1..=ADDABLE_KINDS`. If the new part declares an output that the body did not declare, `part_wires`
connections into that output follow: each with a source chosen evenly from the declared inputs and
the hidden neurons, and a weight of `gauss * new_conn_sigma`. This is rule S2 of M3 for outputs. A
Jaw has no input, so without it a new Jaw would arrive with no wire into `lunge`, and the draft showed
that such a wire is never recruited (C21).

**F1. Debris.** `World::kill(slot, cause, killer)` adds a piece at the body's place, if `corpse_share`
is above 0 and the pool has room:
`energy = corpse_share * (max(energy, 0) + build_cost * mass)`. A full pool loses it. `debris::step`
runs after `plant::step`: each piece keeps `1 - debris_decay` of its energy and is freed under
`debris_min`. In `energy::contact`, after the plants, a Filter takes from each piece it touches, in
grid order: `take = min(budget, room, energy)`, from the same budget and room as the plants.
`stats.debris_eaten += take`. A piece has radius `DEBRIS_RADIUS` (2, a constant of the draw, like
`plant::RADIUS_MIN`).

## The switch-on rule

As in M3. Every new rule has a parameter, and Task 2 gives each its Off value. **Until Task 9 the
default world must play out exactly as at the M3 exit: the rows of `gate-health` and `gate-evolve`
digit for digit.** The state hash changes, because the snapshot layout changes.

What this asks of the code:

- **Same draws.** No new draw is made at an Off value. The add-part mutation draws from
  `ADDABLE_KINDS` (6) until Task 9. A founder makes the Jaw draw only when `founder_jaw` is above 0.
- **Same ports.** `brain::ports` returns the M3 layout until Task 9, so the block of every brain and
  the draws of the add-connection mutation are the M3 ones.
- **Same arithmetic.** At `shell_drag` 0 the forward drag is `drag_fwd`. With no jaw on any body the
  overlap loop does what it does today.
- **A task tests its rule by setting its parameter, and by giving a body a part with `set_size`, in the
  test.** It does not change a default.

Task 9 ends the rule on purpose: the ports change and new kinds can be drawn. It records its rows as
the new baseline. That is a reading, not a trial.

## Global constraints

All M3 constraints hold: `crates/sim` has no dependencies, no standard-library transcendental math,
ascending slot order, no `HashMap` in `crates/sim`, every tunable number in `params.rs`, every field
that affects the future in `save` and `load`. Commit after each task. Do not push in the middle of the
plan. A step that fails 3 times stops and reports. One simulation on the machine at a time, with
`--param` flags written out and the exit code of each step echoed. The load average beside every
timing. One agent drives a browser at a time, on a free port, with the tab in front. A new view gets
its empty state and its error state first. TypeScript: no `any`, no `!` on a value that can be
absent, no `console.log`. Do not run `cargo fmt --all` or `make fmt` in a task.

Tuning happens only in Task 12, at 12,000 ticks or more, one run at a time, against a baseline, with
the hypothesis written before the run.

**About the code in this plan.** Signatures, layouts, names, commands, parameter names, and test
assertions are binding. Function bodies and test bodies are guidance and were not compiled.

**Known soft spots.** Read these before approving.

1. **The bench may go void.** Predators are likely to hold fewer bodies in the bench world, and a mean
   under 1,200 fails `make gates` whatever the speed. The throughput target already has no margin
   (`m3-tuning.md`). Choice C9 must be answered before the end of stage 1 can pass.
2. **Coexistence may fail at tick 144,000 with the trial cap spent.** The draft's boom and bust was
   structural: a payoff cut did not damp it, and throttles drove predators extinct (C14, C15). This
   plan has one restoring force, the stop at full (C12), and no payoff that falls as hunters become
   common. If retune rule 5 stops, the next step is a rule, which is the owner's.
3. **Every Start value is arithmetic.** The bite rate rests on a contact length that Task 1 measures
   for the first time. If contacts last 2 ticks, nothing dies.
4. **A hunter founder can eat its own brood.** A child is born behind its parent and touching it. The
   jaw faces forward, so a child is out of the cone at birth, but it may not stay so. Task 1's
   `kin_share` and `probe-hunt` will show it. No kin rule is planned.
5. **The founders' hunting and fleeing are hand-wired.** At tick 0 a trigger fires in context because
   we wired it. "Evolution keeps it" is a later reading against tick 0, and "evolution builds it" is
   not claimed. The exit's "learned to dash" can be met only in the first sense.
6. **Debris adds food.** A recycled corpse raises what the world can hold, and the draft found that
   more food killed predators (C8). `corpse_share` is a lever in both directions.
7. **The toxin gland may have no niche.** A cloud costs its maker and drains its kin too. Stage 2 has 3
   trials and a stop, and it is deleted alone if it fails.
8. **Task 2 is large.** It touches every file of the core, as M3's Task 1 did. The M3 rows are its test.
9. **Owner stops are many.** Eleven open choices, a motor stop, provisional bands, two stage ends. The
   build runs through three of them under "How the build runs".
10. **A Shell plate makes a body harder to bite twice:** by blocking and by mass under
    `bite_mass_ratio`. That may make shells too strong. The retune reads the mean shell size.
11. **Thirty new parameters.** The retune has 12 trials. Most rows will stay at their Start value
    untuned. The exit's clean-up lists which ones no gate reads.
