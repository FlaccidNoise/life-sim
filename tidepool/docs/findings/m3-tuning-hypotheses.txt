== Part 5, item 2: the egg range (2026-10-14) ==
Change, on a scratch tree only: EGG_RANGE in crates/sim/src/genome.rs from (0.2, 0.7) to
(0.2, 0.9). The founder's egg stays 0.7 (birth_growth). Every other value as Part 4 leaves it,
with mix_rate=1 as a flag. Read: probe-census on seeds 1, 4, 5, 6, 8 to tick 144,000, every
36,000, for egg and len, against the same census at the range as it is.
Hypothesis: the egg sits against the top of its range (0.64 to 0.68 against 0.7 in M2) because
selection pushes it up: a larger newborn survives better than more small ones. If so, with room
above, the mean egg at tick 144,000 rises above 0.7, to 0.72 or more on at least 4 of 5 seeds.
len is the gene of the adult and the egg sets only the newborn's growth, so len stays in its band
(7.8 to 22.2) on 5 of 5 and within 1.5 of the reading at the range as it is.
If the egg stays at 0.70 or below on most seeds, the top of the range was not what held it there,
and the range as it is (or the egg as a parameter) is the proposal.
Confound: the step of a life mutation is mut_sigma * (max - min), so the egg's step grows from
0.040 to 0.056. A rise could be faster drift as well as selection.

== Part 4, trial 1: conn_upkeep up one step, with mixing on (2026-10-14) ==
Readings before it, with --param mix_rate=1: gate-health 3/3, gate-evolve 3/3 at tick 36,000, the
long gate 5/5 with every brain band. Target 2 fails: bench-wasm 2,586 and 2,622 at 1,483
creatures, against 3,023 and 3,017 at 1,357 with mix_rate 0, in turn, load 2.8 to 3.2.
2,604 x 1,483 = 3.86M creature-ticks per second with mixing, 3,020 x 1,357 = 4.10M without: the
cost is +9% bodies and +6% per body. The brains of the bench world are alike (seed 1 at tick
13,000: conns 57.9 against 56.6, hidden genes 4.32 against 4.01), so the premise of rule 3 (that
connections are the cost) does not hold here. More births and the crosses are the likely cost
(births_late 2,334 against 1,935 on seed 1).
Change: conn_upkeep 0.00005 -> 0.0001 (one step: doubled), with mix_rate=1. Rule 3 of Task 10
names this lever first for target 2. Nothing else.
Hypothesis: a founder pays 0.003 more per tick, about 4% more upkeep, so the bench world holds about
4% fewer bodies and runs about 4% faster: 2,700 to 2,750. I expect it to sit at the line, not
clear it, since the brain is not where the cost of mixing is.
Order of readings: gate-health, then the bench (target 2, the aim of the trial), paired in turn
with the mix_rate=1 baseline, twice each, at a load under 5. Stop if the trial reads under 2,700.
Then gate-evolve at 36,000 and the long gate.

== Part 4, trial 2: fixed_upkeep up, with mixing on (2026-10-14) ==
Trial 1 read: gate-health 3/3, bench 2,839 and 2,826 at 1,404 creatures against 2,642 and 2,603
at 1,483 for the mix_rate=1 baseline in turn (load 2.6 to 2.9), gate-evolve 3/3 at 36,000, and the
long gate 4/5: seed 5 fails the scent gap at 0.088. So target 2 is met by a cost of about 0.003 per
tick on a founder, and a cost on wires loses the sense on one seed of five.
Change: fixed_upkeep 0.0337 -> 0.037, with mix_rate=1. conn_upkeep back at 0.00005. Nothing else.
The same 0.0033 per tick on a founder as trial 1, put on the body and not on the wires.
Hypothesis: the cost of mixing is the count of bodies, and any cost of about this size cuts it.
So the bench world holds about 1,400 bodies, as in trial 1, and the bench reads 2,750 or more.
A fixed cost does not price a wire, so the scent sense keeps what it keeps with mixing on and no
trial: the long gate 5/5 (that reading was 5/5, least scent gap 0.10 on seed 1). A fixed cost is a
larger share of the upkeep of a small body, so len at tick 144,000 reads at or above the no-trial
reading (8.9 to 11.0).
Order: gate-health, the bench paired with the mix_rate=1 baseline twice each at a load under 5
(stop if under 2,700), gate-evolve at 36,000, the long gate.
